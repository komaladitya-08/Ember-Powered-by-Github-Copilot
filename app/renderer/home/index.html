      Before Ember starts

      • It reads the window you're working in through the desktop's accessibility service, and for tasks it may take a screenshot of it. App windows on your never-look list and password prompts are never read. The list is in settings, and common password managers are on it already.
      • When you ask something, your request and what Ember read from the screen go to GitHub Copilot to work out the answer. In Project work, Send selected files shares your request and the contents of those files with the connected model. It also shares the previous request and answer if you choose to include them. Nothing is sent before you ask.
      • It asks before acting in an app, and always before anything hard to undo. It never types passwords. Ctrl+Alt+X stops everything at once.Stop everything, in Ember's handle menu and in settings, stops everything at once.
      • On this computer it keeps your settings, logs and a record of every task and action it took, including what you asked for and how it went (kept 30 days), in ~/.config/ember. The rest of your conversation with Ember is kept only while it runs, unless you turn on “Keep my recent conversation” under Conversation: then its latest messages stay on this computer, for you to read, until you turn it off, start a new conversation, sign out or choose Forget everything. If you save a project workspace, Ember encrypts its selected paths and last completed request and answer on this computer until you forget the workspace. It sends no usage statistics.
      • The microphone stays off unless you turn on “Hey Ember” in settings or choose Listen once for one request. Once you call Ember it keeps listening through the conversation, and what it hears there goes to Copilot as text, without its name, until you say “That's all” or it has been quiet for 30 seconds. Listen once processes audio on this computer, sends the request as text, and closes temporary capture before Ember answers. If you teach Ember your voice, it keeps your voiceprint and keeps refining it from requests it is sure are yours (numbers, never audio), and it keeps the words you correct when it mishears you, on this computer until you forget them.
      • Other computers and commands are off until you turn them on: Ember can work on computers you reach over SSH, and run commands in its own window. Each asks you first.

                    Voice
                    
      
                    
        
        
                    
          
                    Ember will keep the microphone open while it runs, listening only for its name (“Ember”, or “Hey Ember”) and “Ember, stop”. That listening happens on this computer: no audio leaves it, and nothing is recorded. After you say “Ember”, what you say next is turned into text on this computer, and only that text is sent to Copilot. After that the microphone stays open for the conversation: what you say next, without its name and also while Ember talks or works, is turned into text and sent too, until you say “That's all” or 30 seconds after everything has gone quiet. Voice settings can turn this off. If you teach Ember your voice, it keeps your voiceprint and keeps refining it from requests it is sure are yours (numbers, never audio), and it keeps the words you correct when it mishears you, on this computer until you forget them. To tell its own voice and your speakers apart from you, Ember also listens to what your speakers play (on this computer only), which keeps the sound output from going idle while Ember listens. During a conversation Ember also checks how loud your computer's sound is (nothing else, and only on this computer), so a song or a video isn't taken for you. GNOME shows a microphone icon while Ember listens. Ctrl+Alt+M turns the microphone off and on.Mute microphone, in Ember's handle menu, turns the microphone off and on.
