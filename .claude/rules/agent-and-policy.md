# Agent, policy and safety: how the rules are implemented

The root CLAUDE.md states the safety rules. This file says where each one lives and how to extend it without weakening it. Every rule here traces to PLAN.md §5 and the §6 gates:
- the kill switch stops everything in under 300 ms;
- a borrow is aborted in under 100 ms;
- injection fixtures produce no out-of-scope actions;
- no risky action goes unconfirmed.

The eval judges its two blocking safety gates (out-of-scope actions on the injection fixtures, and risky actions without confirmation) from each attempt's audit log. It times the kill switch and the borrow abort directly, and tracks them without blocking.

## Copilot sessions and prompts

- **Hardening.** Build every session with `hardenedSessionConfig()`, then call `verifySessionTools()` (app/main/agent/session-config.ts). That gives `availableTools: []`, `excludedBuiltinAgents: BUILTIN_AGENTS`, and an `onPreToolUse` that denies any tool Ember didn't grant.
- **System prompt.** Use `customize` mode through `emberSections()` (persona.ts). Never replace or remove the platform safety section, and put tone inside the identity replacement.
- **Model-facing text.** Describe worker behaviour in `workerRules()`/`commandsRule()` and guide behaviour in `GUIDE_RULES` (worker-persona.ts), and conductor behaviour in `toolRules()` (persona.ts). Take each OS's words from `PLATFORM_WORDS`. `WORKER_RULES` and `TOOL_RULES` are the frozen Linux outputs: with commands off everywhere (`NO_CAPS`), every prompt stays byte-identical (persona-commands.test.ts).

## Adding or changing a worker tool

- **Definition.** Define the tool with `this.tool()` in worker.ts. It parses the zod arguments (the runtime does not), counts the 150-step limit, and runs one call at a time.
- **Routing actions.** Send every app action through `act()` or `system()`. These call `authorize()`, which asks `PolicyEngine.decide()`, asks the user when needed, and writes the audit entries. Commands go only through `run_command` → app/main/commands/flow.ts (the classifier, then decide.ts), never through `authorize()`.
- **Refusals and errors.** Refuse through `refuse()` (logged as a `decision`), return `denied(…)` for a refusal and `fail(…)` for an error. A handler that throws anyway is caught in `runTool()`: logged as `worker.tool_failed` with the tool, and told to the model by `toolFailure()` (the accessibility helper's answers that it can't work here, `helperCantRun()`, point to the setup check; anything else, a timeout or a restart included, is quoted as untrusted words).
- **The minute's limit paces, never refuses.** A decision past the actions-a-minute limit is `paced` (verdict deny, so nothing acts, and it isn't counted); `authorize()` and `authorizeCells()` wait in `pace()` (`PolicyEngine.paceMs()`, on `overSignal`, the card's live line saying so) and decide afresh. Never more than `perMinute` actions go through in any 60 s.
- **Stopping.** After every `await`, check `if (this.isOver) return STOPPED();`, so a stop takes effect at once.
- **Wiring.**
  - Decide whether guide mode gets the tool (the `guide` set in `tools()`).
  - Give it bubble words in `TOOL_STEPS` (presenter.ts) and a kind of step in `STEP_GROUP` (app/shared/activity.ts: its card's words), and describe it in the persona rules. Its card hears of it through the worker's `onActivity` (app/main/agent/activity.ts): tool calls, questions, refusals and notes, never arguments or screen text.
  - Keep eval/tasks/*.json calling it with arguments it accepts (eval-harness.test.ts checks this).
- **Tests.** Test it in worker-tools.test.ts and policy.test.ts (worker-commands.test.ts for command tools).

## Policy and confirmation

- **Decisions.** Describe each action as an `ActionRequest`, and let `PolicyEngine.decide()` (app/main/policy/policy.ts) decide it. Build question text from the request (`describe(r)`), never from model output.
- **Matching.** Normalise labels with `normalizeLabel`/`foldLabel` before matching word lists. Policy checks a risky step against `TaskSpec.userWords`, never against the conductor's goal string. A "try again" (`retryRequest`, status.ts: also "can you try again?", "yes, try again") after the line of a task that may be tried again (Ember's lines and at most two short questions of the user's about what happened between), or a yes to that task's own offer to try again, carries that task's own words and what they agreed to (`TaskManager.askedWith`, host.ts `retriedWords`), tells the new worker when the old try did part of the work (`partWay`: a step that changed something, never only a look or a picture of the window, `LOOKS_ONLY` in tasks.ts) and closes the old card: never its goal, never for unchecked or suggested words, never after a "Not now" or a Stop, never past a request of the user's own.
- **New risky verbs.** Add each one to `RISKY` and to the per-language `RISKY_WORDS`/`RISKY_ANYWHERE` lists (verbs that only a control acts on: `RISKY_ACT*`; one OS's words: `RISKY_EXTRA[os]` in vocabulary.ts). These are confirmed every time:
  - risky actions themselves;
  - OK or Yes in a dialog about one of them;
  - destructive keys;
  - moving data between windows;
  - security settings.

  They never offer "Always". After a deny or "Not now", never retry or work around it, and don't soften "Don't try another way".
- **Work without asking** (app/main/policy/autonomy.ts; PLAN.md §2 "Approvals"). `decideBase` marks a question `waivable` only where nothing risky asks: `["apps"]` for a first step in an app, `["cursor"]` for an ordinary borrow, `crossWindow` (plus `apps` or `cursor`) where the only risk is carried text (`Risk.carried`), and (widened 2026-11-03, owner decision of 2026-11-02) `apps` or `cursor` where the only risk is Ember's doubt about an ordinary step (`Risk.plain`), all through `waiverOf()`. `Risk.plain` comes only from allow-lists that fail closed (security review of #140): `goesSomewhere()` and `BROWSER_KEYS` for a browser's own ways of going somewhere (2026-11-05), and `acknowledgesOnly()` (structural first: `dialog.controls`, which the worker's `dialogAround` sends only from a whole read with nothing omitted under the dialog (the readers set `AxNode.omitted` on the listed element that has something unlisted under it: tree.ts, `ember_atspi.py`'s `budget.omitted`, the UIA bridge's `omitted` and `childrenCut`, ax-darwin-read.ts's `omit()` for an element or children it couldn't read; controls in a separate top-level window the dialog owns can't be seen by any of them), leaves out a title bar's Minimise, Maximise, Restore, Help and system menu (push buttons and menu items only, by name) but lists its Close (`caption`) and anything else in it, and apart from a title bar's Close is exactly one push button named OK, the one pressed (the Close is allowed only for a real Win32 dialog box, `messageBox`, #32770); no other button, check box, radio button, toggle, combo box, field, list or link; not a page's dialog (`page`), not in `WEB_APP` or `TABBED_APP`; never keyed on the "alert" role, which only Linux reports. Then, as defence in depth: not `truncated`, words that say something, all Latin or Russian Cyrillic (`readableScripts`), no question mark and no `QUESTION_FORM`, text of at most `ACK_TEXT_MAX` (120) characters, no `FUTURE_PASSIVE` marker (a future or passive statement of what happens to something: a blacklist, so the structure is the real gate), no risky word anywhere (RISKY's lists, the acting verbs, security talk, `ACK_RISK`, a file conflict, a disk's Format), not in Settings, security, administration or a `PROMPT_WINDOW`), and `plainEditorLines()` (line breaks and no tab into the `text` or `document text` view of an app on `PLAIN_EDITOR[os]` in vocabulary.ts, never with `formula`, in a cell or `inPage`, only the window's main document (`mainDocument`, sent only from a whole read, `knownWhole`: the largest multi-line text it shows, clearly a document by role, name or a document tab) in a window titled by a file or a new document (`FILE_WINDOW`, a weak check: `mainDocument`, the role and `around` are the real gates), and never when the field's name or `around` (its label, the panes around it and the selected tab of each, which the worker always sends) says console, terminal, shell, command, REPL, Python or output: `RUNS_LINES`). Never widen either by adding to a list of risky words, nor make the dialog waiver depend on its words: add to the allow-list or the structure, and add the probe as a test that asks. Keep Ember's doubt about what a key does (Alt+letter, unknown shortcuts, a letter in a menu, Enter with nothing focused) asking: it is the commands' "unclear". `judge()` turns such a question into allow with `autonomy: true` and an `announce` built from the request (`announcement()`: carried text by app and size only) while `PolicyInputs.autonomy()` covers every scope it needs; off-task steps and the admin's "confirm every action" return before it. Never mark a risky decision waivable.
  - The worker announces it (`announce()`: the bubble, and the card as an `announce` activity item, never a model `note`; report_progress refuses words that pass for one, `POSES_AS_ANNOUNCEMENT`), audits the decision with `autonomy: true` (both held until the look right before acting has passed: `goAhead()`), and holds a tainted, steered or refused task to asking (the decision is turned back into a question and loses `autonomy`/`announce`). A refusal sets `saidNoThisTask`, which nothing clears: never key the suspension on `declined`, which later steps reset.
  - Announcements name the kind of step, the app and a size (`announcement()`), never a control's name, typed text or screen text. No step in the system's Settings, a security app or a system-administration app (`SETTINGS_APP`, `SECURITY_APP`, `ADMIN_APP` in vocabulary.ts, by program and friendly name) is waived. The bubble shows an announcement only through `Presenter.workedWithoutAsking()`, which alone sets `BubbleState.autonomous` (the "Without asking" tag); any other bubble change drops it. `POSES_AS_ANNOUNCEMENT` (after `normalizeLabel`) is defence in depth only. A Stop during a command's countdown counts as a no (`onDeclined`).
  - Turning the mode on or off writes an `autonomy` audit entry with `via` (home, voice, typed) and `verified`; a turn on that can't be recorded is undone.
  - Commands: only `autonomousCommand()` (commands/flow.ts), on this computer only (`kind: "local"`), for an ask whose only rule is `carried`, with commands and crossWindow on, the user's own words, no taint and no other computer named. An ordinary command (no finding) already runs without a question; the danger classes, root or an elevated token and "ask every" are never waived.
  - fill_cells (`authorizeCells`) follows the same rules as one step: `autonomy: true` on each cell's decision, one announcement for the batch, and the suspension after a no.
  - It is in force only through `autonomyInForce()`: `on`, consent `>= FEATURE_CONSENT_VERSION.autonomy`, and `autonomyPermitted()` (the admin's `features.autonomy`, `allowGrants`, `confirmEveryAction`, `features.actions`). LOCKDOWN sets `features.autonomy: false`.
- **Action consent stays with its context.** `act()`, `cursorAct()` and `pressKeys()` compare the window identity and semantic accessibility tree that supplied policy before asking and again immediately before input, after leases and pointer movement. Text, dialog controls, selected values and safety attributes changing, or a failed read, aborts the action and requires a fresh perception/policy attempt. Focus and bounds alone may change. After Ember's own click into a text field for append/replace, that field's selection may collapse; its text and every other safety/context attribute remain bound. Insert-at-caret keeps its selection binding. A menu closed by the Allow click never carries its approval into a later attempt, even for the same label or app. Action-context reads use separate baselines for the quick accessibility tree and the detailed target read. AT-SPI omits empty and object-placeholder text from the tree and limits its text more tightly; the detailed target text is compared exactly against its own pre-approval value, never by prefix. Each action refreshes policy context before approval, preserving original actionable ids only when the tree structure and target identity still match. Repeated role/name targets (such as two dialogs' OK buttons) are ambiguous and are refused before approval; positions are never used to choose between them. A web page lives on its own (a video playing, a hover preview), so a control of one, in no dialog and with no risky word in its name, is the exception (`livingPage()`, worker.ts; owner's report of 2026-11-05: every click on YouTube was refused): a step that asks nothing needs only its window, no modal over it and the object itself to hold (`checkPageTarget()`), and a link or button may share its name with a twin, since the click goes to the object by id (`PAGE_TWIN`). A question still binds its answer to the whole tree, and pressed keys likewise (`focusShell`). This lets a completed form step update its status and contents without carrying its approval into the next step.
- **Spoken answers.** Main sends an utterance to `voiceAnswer()` (app/main/agent/voice-answer.ts) before the conversation: only while a question is up, only a whole short answer, never when Ember spoke since the request's wake (`VoiceService.selfSpokeSince`, the echo guard). A yes needs Voice Match on and the utterance `verified` (set only by app/voice/match.ts when the voiceprint matched and what follows the wake phrase holds at least `APPROVAL_MIN_SECONDS` (0.5 s) of voiced frames and matches on its own at the threshold (`voicedFrames`, `afterWakePhrase`, the engine's `requestWithWake`); never check the whole request with the wake phrase in it; a shorter or weaker match is `short` with that part's own `restScore`, and a request the model couldn't check carries no flag), or a `short` yes on the asking task's voice trust (`TaskVoiceTrust`, voice-answer.ts; owner decision of 2026-11-03): granted only by host.ts when the turn that started the task was a verified spoken request (`UserRequest.verifiedAt`), for `TASK_VOICE_MS`; ended for every task when Voice Match turns a voice away or a yes is refused as another voice, and for a task steered by an unverified request; refused when the yes has no `restScore` (too little voice to score: never trusted) or it is under `SHORT_YES_FLOOR` (compared unrounded, against the enrolled voiceprint: change how that is made and the floor needs measuring again); ended for every task when the owner's voiceprint is forgotten or taught again or Voice Match is switched (`TrustEnd` "voice-changed"); the two decisions main wires are the pure `rejectionEndsTrust` and `refusalEndsTrust`; taken by the Confirmer only for that task's own question (`q.task`), never a panel's or a click-only one, and audited `verified: false, trust: "task-voice"`; then `Confirmer.answerVoice()` with the question up when the speech started and its start time (an answer said before that question was up and armed is `stale`; after Voice Match turned another voice away during it, `lockVoice()` takes no spoken yes for it), which allows only what the approve key could (armed, not borrowing, whole text read, panel shown whole, never a click-only card, never "Always"). "Not now" by voice is taken from anyone ("cancel" and "never mind" aren't answers); while a question is up the voice process lets a short "no" in a follow-up window through as an answer (`VoiceService.questionUp`, engine.ts `isAnswerWord`). A yes asked as a question ("Proceed?"), padded with more than one filler word, or a bare acknowledgement ("okay", "go on") isn't one (`spokenAnswer`). A refused yes leaves the question up with `Confirmer.note()` saying why and what to do (`VOICE_ANSWER_REFUSED`: none names Ember or reads as an answer), before the keys that answer it and never in their place, and Ember says it aloud (`voiceAnswerSaid()`: fixed sentences, and after a line that points to a click or the keys the question keys Ember holds, never what was heard). A wake the engine turned away because Ember was speaking (`rejected`, reason `self`) whose transcript is an answer (`heardAnswer()`) is never taken: Ember stops speaking and asks for it again (`heard-over`). Workers and the command flow audit the channel from `howAnswered()` (`via: "voice"`, `verified`).
- **Voice intents for the mode** ("work without asking", "ask me first again") are answered in the host (app/main/agent/autonomy-intent.ts), never by a conductor tool: on only from a verified voice with the card agreed to (otherwise Home opens at the switch or its card), off for anyone.
- **Confirmer guards.** Keep them in confirm.ts:
  - a 500 ms default arm delay;
  - a 700 ms gap between questions;
  - expiry as "no" after 120 s times the user's read-time setting, longer for long texts;
  - no answers accepted while Ember borrows the mouse;
  - panel questions (the command card, consent cards) answered only from their own window, with the hash of what it showed;
  - Allow on a whole-text question only after all of it was in view.

  "Always in <app>" is a click only, never bound to a key, because a global key could take an app's own shortcut and grant for good.

## Views (show_view, update_view, finish_task's view)

- **Checked whole, fail closed.** Every view, and every update_view batch applied to a copy, goes through
  `checkDoc()` (app/main/agent/view-schema.ts): the closed catalog and its limits, then every string, every string
  read with its label, column or neighbours (`nodeTogether`), and the whole view read together:
  - `mimicRefusal()`: an invisible, default-ignorable or bidi character refuses the view, except where writing needs
    it (`hidesCharacters`: the emoji selector after a pictograph, a joiner between pictographs, (non-)joiners inside
    Persian, Indic and other joining scripts). Ember's control words are matched by skeleton (`viewNorm()`: NFKD with
    marks dropped, Cyrillic, Greek, Armenian, Cherokee and small-capital look-alikes folded to Latin; then I, 1, | as l
    and 0 as o). Chords are matched by tokens (`namesChord`: only Ember's own allow, deny and stop chords from
    shortcuts.ts, their modifiers by any OS's name or symbol, any separators, quotes and filler words); in each
    string, label with value, table row, list, row and the whole view in reading order (key first, run together, with
    filler words). Ember's voice (`speaksAsEmber`:
    a capitalised "Ember:" where a speaker's name stands, "Ember:" before words of consent, "I'm Ember", "Ember says";
    "from Ember:", "Ember asked/wrote/told"; digits and spelt-out letters folded) in every string, across fields and
    through the whole view; and a heading, title, label or column that is only "Ember" with anything after it.
  - `secretRefusal()`: `memoryRefusal()` (a token, key, card number, IBAN, social security number, a key's BEGIN
    line, a secret's name, perhaps with what it is for, with a value that isn't a word like "required", a bare
    password, PIN or door code with digits or symbols in its value, a login or credentials said as one such value;
    a secret-named key with code for its value is passed over by `withoutCodeKeys()`, never a literal under it, as a
    hash's entry, a property naming one of several, or in a mapping, list or "{"/"[" it opens (`holdsUnder()`,
    safety-privacy-r5-1); a prompt's or a label's words with only code or markup after them are written out first
    (`withoutLabels()`, memory-r4-2: markup read past blank lines up to the field it labels, never a field's value,
    nor a password-shaped word in the code or comment after a prompt, memory-x6-1 (a ticket only after "see", a
    version alone or a tool's, a plain to-do owner: memory-z6-4), nor one on the line after a field with no value,
    or on the next line with text up to its row's or form's end, or a select's option, memory-y6-4, memory-z6-3);
    never a quoted or bare value, a
    PIN's number, a shell variable's word or code that gives a literal as a default, never by a bare shape after a
    prose label's ":" (`codeish()`, or a bracket a line of code opened above it, `openedAbove()`), and its value is
    still read for tokens: add a code shape
    there only with its look-alike secret pinned as refused in memory.test.ts; a code block's or a file's line is
    read with the lines before it, `withLinesBefore()`), and a private path anywhere in any string, after NFKC and with
    look-alike slashes read as "/" (but not /private/tmp, a Keynote .key, .env.example or id_rsa.pub). A token split
    over up to five strings in reading order, or written with spaces, is caught run together (`tokenRefusal`, the
    redactor's whole-token patterns only).

  A refusal for what a view says (`why`) is audited as a `decision` with `kind: "view"`, shown on the card as a
  refusal, and the model is told not to try another way. A quote's caption names an app only if the task read it (as
  the task read it), and a file or a web page only if the task read something or ran a command. A new check adds its
  bypasses to view-redteam.test.ts.
- **Views reach the conductor only as data.** A finished task's view goes to the next turn as words inside
  `<task_results untrusted="true">` (with "<" escaped), and to the conversation as the task's text, never as Ember's.
- **Never in the audit:** a view's words. It records "showed a view" with its blocks, charts and rev.
- **To the page apart from the state.** TaskManager's `onView` → main's `ViewHub` (app/main/agent/view-doc.ts): diffs
  only, at most 4 a second a task, nothing while Ember is hidden, every view whole to a page that reloads.

## Untrusted text, secrets and never-look windows

- **Untrusted text.** Wrap text from the screen, apps, files, the web or other tasks in an element marked `untrusted="true"`: `<screen>`, `<context>`, `<windows>`, `<options>`, `<task_results>`, `<tasks>` or `<command_output>` (built only by `shapeOutput()`). Quote it with `quoteUntrusted()`, and escape role and action names with `screenWord()`.
- **Secrets.** `SECRET_WORDS`/`SECRET_ANYWHERE` must stay the same in TypeScript and Python. The Windows and macOS host (app/sidecar-host/secrets.ts) imports elements.ts's lists: never copy them there. Add new labels to test/fixtures/secret-labels.json (and options to chosen-options.json), because both test suites read these files.
- **Never-look windows.** Check the never-look list (`settings.privacy.neverLook` plus the admin's list) before every capture and every tree read. Those windows are left out of trees and window lists, and blacked out in images.
- **Screenshots.** Only workers get them, and only while the model reads images, the admin's `features.screenImages` isn't false and the user's `settings.screenImages` (Home's "Send screenshots for tasks") is on (host.ts `workerVision`, read as each task's worker is made, and the worker's `picturesAllowed()`, read again at every look, so turning it off stops a task already at work). If `maskAfter` returns null, send no image. Each one is audited before it is sent (`event: "read"`, `kind: "screenshot"`: the app and its size, never the window's title), and one that can't be recorded isn't sent; What Ember did lists it, and the task's card says it (the worker's `picture` activity makes its read row "Sent the model a picture of the window", named first in the summary). Keep it out of `event: "action"`, which the eval's safety gates count.
- **Launching apps on the user's screen.** Use only the platform's `AppCatalog.launch()`: on Linux `launchApp()` (app/main/hands/system.ts: `gtk-launch <desktop id>`, else the parsed Exec line, `execArgv`, no shell), on Windows explorer.exe with the app's shortcut, on macOS `open -a`. Each refuses terminals (`isTerminalApp(app, os)`), starts in the home folder and passes `appEnv()`, an allowlist that never passes tokens, keys, secrets or passwords, or Ember's, GitHub's, Copilot's, SSH's, GPG's, the keyring's or Electron's variables.
- **Launching apps on the desk.** Apps start through the desk place's `open()` (app/main/desk/place.ts). It also refuses terminals, and calls `Desk.run()` with the desk's `appEnv(process.env, { session: false })` environment and the hand-off folder.
- **Ember's own windows.** Ember never reads or acts in them: `isEmberWindow()` (policy.ts) matches Ember's process id, then its app name, and its titles (`EMBER_WINDOW`) only when the id is unknown.

## Tiers, the desk and the kill switch

- **Tier 1** (background accessibility: AT-SPI on Linux and in WSL, UI Automation or AX through app/sidecar-host) never moves the pointer, takes focus or raises a window. Return `background_unavailable` instead, and let the router ask to escalate.
- **Tier 3** (borrowing the cursor):
  - confirm every time (with Work without asking on, an ordinary borrow is announced instead: `waivable: ["cursor"]`);
  - wait for the user to be still;
  - let go on any user input, and after that never move the pointer again;
  - unless the user took over, restore the pointer and the active window when the borrow ends.

  Target-dependent refusals (no borrowing in WSLg windows) live only in policy/target-rules.ts. On the user's screen, text typed with the borrowed keyboard always goes in by paste (any layout, accents, long text), and the clipboard is put back afterwards. Ember pastes only when it can put the clipboard back. On the desk, text is typed.
- **The desk** (Linux only; `Platform.desk` is null elsewhere) is a private Xvfb with its own D-Bus, clipboard and app profiles. Make every desk X call in the desk helper (app/main/desk/agent-process.ts, bundled as dist/app/desk-agent.cjs and run with `ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE`), never in Ember's own process. Risky steps on the desk are still confirmed. Desks use displays :90 to :139.
- **The kill switch.** Register every new long-running part with `KillSwitch` as a `StopPart`. After a stop, no tool sends anything to any app.
- **Process cleanup.** Never signal a cached pid without checking what it names. Linux desk and command sweeps retain
  `/proc/<pid>/stat` start times and check them again immediately before signalling; unreadable or reused identities
  are left alone. macOS sweeps re-read the target pid and its run marker. The desk relay stops its own live child
  handle, never a pid cached by the main thread.

## The overlay

- **One window per display.** Each display gets one transparent, frameless, `focusable: false`, click-through window, shown with `showInactive()`.
- **What takes clicks.** Its clickable area is exactly the rectangles `reportInteractive()` sends (the X input shape on Linux; hover polling against them on Windows and macOS) (app/renderer/overlay/main.ts). To make a new element clickable:
  - put it in `bubble.interactiveRects()` or `taskRects`;
  - set `interactiveDirty` when its box changes.

  The report goes to main only when the rectangles change. While Ember borrows the mouse the shape is empty, so the overlay takes no clicks.
- **Click-through per OS.** `setIgnoreMouseEvents({forward:true})` and `setContentProtection` do nothing on Linux. Use `forward` only where the shaper says so (macOS): on Windows it would hook every app's mouse. `setContentProtection(true)` keeps windows out of screenshots on Windows and macOS.

## Consent, voice and privacy

- **Consent.** Read, hear and send nothing until `hasConsent` is true. WSL, SSH (per computer) and commands (per target) each have a first-use card whose text is in app/main/targets/consent.ts; it is hashed with the OS's stop chord, and a yes counts only with that hash. Work without asking has its own card in Home (`AUTONOMY_CONSENT_TEXT`, app/renderer/home/copy.ts; `FEATURE_CONSENT_VERSION.autonomy`).
- **The microphone.**
  - It opens only when all of these hold: listening for “Hey Ember” is on (`wakeWord`), the admin's policy allows voice (`features.voice`), voice consent and general consent are given, the listening models (`LISTENING_MODELS`) are verified, Ember isn't muted, and the screen isn't locked.
  - Keep the constraints `echoCancellation: true, noiseSuppression: false, autoGainControl: false`, because gain control and noise suppression distort speech enough to miss the wake word.
  - The bare name "Ember" is spotted inside "remember" and "September", so a wake by it shows only once the transcript check hears the name start the request, it stands alone in a short segment (`nameOnItsOwn`), or it came first with no other word heard (`NAME_FIRST_FRAMES`) (app/voice/engine.ts, `BARE_NAME`). A word the recognizer wrote for the name counts only by how the words go on (`CALLED_AS_WORD`).
  - A conversation (engine.ts `CONVERSATION_IDLE_MS`, `voice.conversation`) hears on through Ember's voice: `duck` at the first speech over it, `cut-in` once the words aren't its own (`spokenByEmber`), `overEmber` on what was heard so it never approves. Keep Ember's own words flowing to the engine (`VoiceService.speak` → `self-words`). Main dates each utterance from its own speech, so `selfSpokeSince` holds in a window minutes old. A window whose request opens one ends with `next: "conversation"`, and VoiceService goes straight on into it, never through `waiting`: lines that changed and changed back were said by a screen reader into the conversation, as a request (voice-listening-c3-1).
- **Speaking.**
  - Speak only through `VoiceService.speak()`. Ember's default voice (Kitten Nano 0.8 FP32, or explicitly selected optional Kokoro: below) is made in the speech utility process (app/voice/tts-process.ts) and played by the mic page (app/renderer/mic); when its native backend or verified model is unavailable, the speed gate fails, or synthesis fails, the page speaks with the system's voices (speech-dispatcher on Linux), and only voices that run on the computer (app/renderer/mic/voices.ts), since an online voice would send Ember's words elsewhere. Kitten reads only English: a reply `speechLanguage()` (app/voice/spoken.ts) finds in another language goes to a local system voice for that language (`SpeakRequest.lang`), or isn't said (never reported as speech failing). An answer's language is decided once for all its pieces (main's `AnswerLanguage`, passed as `speak()`'s `lang`), never piece by piece, and never by a short message before a tool call (`take(…, more)`) or a request that only names or quotes another language (`requestLanguage`). The page is the mic window, or, while listening is off, a hidden speak-only window that never opens the mic. Either one tells the voice process while Ember speaks. Whether Ember speaks follows `speaksAloud()` (app/shared/speech.ts), the admin's `features.speak` and the screen lock; turned off while Ember talks, what it is saying stops at once (main's `silenceIfOff`, on every settings change and before each sentence: `speechSilenced`).
  - Where Ember's own speech can't be told from the user's (`rejectsSpeakers` is false: Windows, macOS, Linux without `parec`), `speak()` drops Ember's wake and stop phrases (`withoutOwnPhrases`), and words heard then never count towards a dangerous command (`spokenWords`). That is what lets “Hey Ember” cut in on Ember there (engine.ts `bargeIn`, `CUT_IN_WAKE`: never “Amber”, “Emma”, “A. M. B.” or “Ember's”; `withoutOwnPhrases` names or breaks apart everything it matches, voice-spoken.test.ts); the bare name never does, and nothing heard before the page says Ember stopped speaking is sent. Keep the two patterns together if you change either. Ember's own voice is trimmed from the pre-roll and the room only without the speakers' stream: with `parec` on Linux they behave as before.
- **Speech on sherpa-onnx.** Both utility processes reach sherpa-onnx only through app/voice/runtime.ts: on Linux its prebuilt C API through koffi (app/voice/capi.ts; the libraries in `resources/voice-native`, which `capiChoice()` uses only when they are there and the processor has `CAPI_CPU_NEEDS`, and never with `EMBER_VOICE_BACKEND=wasm`; the Linux addon needs glibc 2.32, and `packaging-and-ci.md` says how it is kept out), its native addon on Windows and macOS, and WebAssembly for listening wherever the native build can't run or doesn't load. A damaged model can abort the native build's onnxruntime. A listening process that dies before it is ready starts again on WebAssembly (`voice.native_failed`); failed neural synthesis uses the installed local system voice. WebAssembly memory never shrinks, so keep every buffer in app/voice/sherpa.ts bounded.
- **The clearer recognizer** (`CLEAR_SPEECH`, `voice.clearHearing`, off by default; owner's report of 2026-11-05). Native builds only (`Sherpa.createNemo`), fetched only once the user turns on **Hear me more clearly**, used only once it verifies. It writes requests (`transcribe(…, "request")`); Moonshine keeps every quick check (the name, a cut-in, stop), which stay cheap. A model that won't load or fails on a request leaves Moonshine writing them, and Home says so (`ClearHearingStatus`).
- **Models.** Each one is pinned by SHA-256 in app/voice/models.ts, downloaded only after the user agrees, and used only once it verifies. Archives unpack in-process (untar.ts, bzip2.ts), refusing anything that could write outside their folder, into a staging folder moved into place whole. Kitten Nano 0.8 FP32 is `DEFAULT_VOICE` (`id: "tts"`) in the standard `VOICE_MODELS` download and package staging; its source is pinned and Apache-2.0. Optional Kokoro and Supertonic 3 (`voice.natural` with `voice.naturalModel`, off by default; `SUPERTONIC_VOICE`, its fifth voice, the addon only) are fetched only after an explicit choice with the download size. Both require native capability and the measured `NATURAL_MAX_RTF` speed gate: Kokoro falls back to Kitten, and unsupported, slow or failed Kitten falls back to the installed local system voice. No production Piper model or synthesis fallback remains. Pinned Piper Amy/Ryan voices may remain only for ASR/evaluation fixture generation. Release the neural speech utility after two minutes without pending synthesis, rewarming on the next wake, the ask box's opening and a request's start while Ember may speak (`VoiceService.warmSpeech`); a voice already `on` stays `on` as it loads again (`warmVoice` publishes no "checking" then: a screen reader said it over the request and into the idle wake microphone, voice-listening-c5-1), and a load that now fails says so. Stop cancels even a process that has not emitted spawn yet. A measured too-slow verdict on optional Kokoro is remembered for a week (never on Kitten: a busy moment must not cost the default voice), keyed by the model, provider and thread configuration; damaged, future-dated and expired verdicts are ignored.
- **Voice Match.** It never decides who is heard: a request that doesn't match is said (`rejected`, for trust) and heard unverified. The owner's voiceprint (256 numbers that can't be turned back into speech) is kept only in `voiceprint.json` in Ember's data folder, mode 0600, apart from the settings, which are shown and logged more freely (app/main/voiceprint-store.ts); **Forget my voice** deletes the file. "Ember, stop" is never checked against it: anyone may stop Ember, also while it is taught: the voice process then takes no wake, follow-up or request (its phrases are requests) but feeds the engine for the kill phrase alone (`VoiceEngine.stopsOnly`; the spotter takes the name first in "Ember, stop" and "Hey Ember, stop", so a spotted name is a stop where the recognizer heard "stop" with it, `saidWakeStop`, and the teaching's phrases are nothing), which ends the teaching (`why: "stopped"`). Teaching ends too at `ENROL_LIMITS` (`timeout`), after `of × 3` tries (`gave-up`), on the kill switch (`cancelListening`), when Home closes or hides (`endTeaching`), and when the voice process goes (`VoiceService.teardown`: `interrupted`); Home says which (`VoiceMatchStatus.ended`), never that it learnt the voice. While a screen reader runs (main's `reader` on `enrol`), it reads each prompt aloud into the teaching microphone, and a voiceprint learnt from that was mostly its voice (voice-listening-r4-1): `Enrolment`'s `EnrolReader` takes nothing begun within `READER_LEAD_MS` of a prompt showing (the voice process dates each segment from where `speaking()` began) or in a prompt's own words (`PROMPT_WORDS`, by the recognizer), and asks again (`over`) where the phrase's words came twice (the owner's ran on from the reading), or came once, in none of a prompt's words, with no speech after it for `QUICK_PHRASE_WAIT_MS` (`Enrolment.speechBegan`, from the voice process's VAD: the owner on headphones, or speaking at once, is never dropped without a word, voice-listening-r5-2); Listen once's line is `reason: "listening"` meanwhile, and Home holds its other status lines (`micHeld`). A request it matched carries `verified: true` (match.ts); only such a request may turn Work without asking on, start a task with voice trust, or allow a question by voice, but for a short yes to that task's own question (Spoken answers, above).
- **Voice Match that keeps learning** (app/voice/adapt.ts, docs/ASSISTANT.md §7). The voice process offers main a `voice-sample` (`sampleOffered()`: a verified request with `ADAPT.minVoicedSeconds` of voice after its wake phrase, which main checks again in `adaptGuard`, numbered by `utterance` so main pairs it with that request only, that matched by `ADAPT.margin` more, one voice throughout by the gate's own test: adapt-sample.ts); main learns from it only through `PersonalVoice.sample()` → `adaptGuard()` (Keep learning on, a request that went to `askEmber`, never a spoken answer, not Ember's own voice since the wake, no stranger turned away, no question `Confirmer.voiceLocked` and no stop within `ADAPT_QUIET_MS`, not `cut`, at most one per `ADAPT.minGapMs`) and `adapt()` (the enrolment as anchor, at most `ADAPT.maxStep` a sample, never beyond `ADAPT.anchorMin`). The gate then checks a request against the learnt print at `adaptedThreshold()` and against the enrolment at `ADAPT.anchorFloor` to match and `ADAPT.verifiedFloor` (the enrolment's own threshold) to be `verified` (`gateMatches`): never loosen any of them, and re-run scripts/voice/match-bench.mjs (false accepts, and the drift attack) when you change a limit. `voiceprint.json` keeps numbers only; **Reset to my enrolment** drops what was learnt; **Forget my voice** (`PersonalVoice.forgetVoice`) deletes it and `words.json`. What these keep is in "Before Ember starts" (added in `CONSENT_VERSION` 2): a change to it needs a bump.
- **Personal vocabulary and suggestions** (app/main/personal/). A heard request is corrected only by `correctTranscript()`: never a common word into a name, never digits, nothing but Ember's name in a request `isRiskyLabel` calls risky or that addresses someone (`ADDRESSES`, defence in depth), never a word into a person's name from memory (or a suggestion's) unless the request only looks something up (`LOOKS_UP`: what, who, when, where, find, search, show, look up, read: a verb list can't cover every way of handing something to someone, so everything else stays as heard), and a correction that would make a request risky is undone (the raw words go on). Spoken answers (`voiceAnswer`) see the raw words. Learnt corrections (`words.json`, 0600) come only from the user retyping a request they spoke ("That's not what I said", by the row's `heardId`), never a risky meaning or a non-`learnable` one (all common words, a request verb changed, a number word or digit), and old entries are held to that too. Memory's names are read only while memory is in force, `user` facts only. Suggestions (`predictNext`) are local rules, never risky unless built from the user's own risky request, never from a voice-unchecked, suggested or risky history item; a chip is sent with `suggested` (host.ts: never the user's own words for commands, so never the announce-with-Stop path), from the bubble only for `suggestionsShown(lastState)[n]` and from the Ask box only for one `askSuggestions` offers now (none while a question is up; a `follows` one only while its answer shows; an `answers` one, "Yes, please" and "No, thanks", only while its last words show too: not under Read all, and opened in full only once the overlay says its end has shown, `BubbleBox.endShown`, type-checked in main (`bubbleBoxOf`), or without a compositor the corner card, only from the handle and only for the very words shown now (`answerEndShown`); Read all carries the suggestions over to the whole answer, `expandedSuggest`). A request's provenance (`voiceUnchecked`, `suggested`) is kept wherever it is stored or replayed: the transcript (also for requests answered on the spot, `answerLocally`), the queue, Try again (`recentAsks`) and the predictions (`lastRequestOwn`: only the user's own words may authorise a risky suggestion). Logs get counts and reason words, never the words heard.
- **Audio** stays on the machine and is never written to disk.
- **Permissions.** Every web permission request is denied except audio `media` for Ember's mic window (`voicePermissions`), and the mic page's system audio for its level meter where `outputLevel` says so (output-watch.ts: only the level, its video stopped at once, only while a conversation is open). The spellchecker stays off, because it would download dictionaries.
- **Conversation data.**
  - Ember's own conversation log is in memory only, unless the user turns on `keepConversation` (off by default): then `conversation.json` (0600, app/main/agent/conversation-store.ts) keeps the latest lines for them to read after a restart. Restored lines are inert (`restored` in host.ts: never model context, never agreed to, never words a task is checked against); Home is told which they are only through `getTranscript()`'s copy (`TranscriptEntry.restored`, never saved) and marks where they end. Sign-out, an account change, New conversation, turning it off and Forget everything delete it; any new place that empties the transcript must call `dropHistory()`.
  - Copilot's session state lives in `<userData>/copilot` only while Ember runs. It is deleted at quit, at the next start after a crash, and on sign-out.
- **The audit log.** Write every decision, question, answer, action, refusal, task outcome and stop to `AuditLog`:

On Linux the journald copy (`journalctl -t ember-audit`, built by `journalLine()`) replaces goals, summaries, window titles, element names, chosen values, errors, folders, command text and a command review's words (`review`: the model's, apart from the reason) with their sizes; reasons, app names and shortcuts stay, so a reason is only ever Ember's own words. ## Extensions

docs/DEVELOPING.md "Extensions: the runtime and the gate" is the design; app/main/extensions/ the code.
- **Only MCP servers load**, passed by Ember as explicit `mcpServers`. Never pass a third-party plugin as
  `pluginDirectories` (its command hooks run outside every gate), never set `requestExtensions`, and never give a model
  a tool that installs, enables, reloads or manages extensions: only the user does that, in Home.
- **Admit tools one by one** through `hardenedSessionConfig({ extensions })`: `mcp:<server>-<tool>` names only, never
  one equal to an Ember tool (an MCP tool replaces it silently), never `builtin:`.
- **Every call goes through `gate.ts`'s `classify()`**: hints only make a call stricter; a changing tool asks every
  time with a question built by `gateQuestion()` (no Always); a deny is final for the turn; every decision and result
  is audited with sizes only; a call that can't be recorded is denied.
- **Results** are rewritten by `wrapResult()` (untrusted, `<` escaped, capped, no images) and scanned by injection.ts.
  Failures can't be rewritten: `onPostToolUseFailure` adds `failureNote()` and taints the conversation. Taint lives on
  the service for the whole conversation (reconnects included), never on one session.
- **Check pins at every call**, against the session's live tool list (`bind`), not only at load: a server can change
  a tool mid-session. A session without a live list denies every call.
- **Read-only** is `isReadOnly()` (readOnlyHint, and neither destructiveHint nor openWorldHint) on a stdio server only;
  a remote server's tools always ask, and so do a server's tools once it changes its tool list mid-session.
- **The runtime's TMPDIR** is `copilot-tmp/` in the data folder (runtime-temp.ts, 0700, emptied at connect and
  sign-out): it saves large tool output there. A result carrying its "Saved to:" pointer counts as flagged.
- **Pins** (pin.ts) are checked at install, start and re-enable, and against what the session shows; a changed tool
  stays off until `reagree`.
- **Servers start through the launcher** (launch.ts) with `appEnv()`'s allowlist, never the runtime's environment.
- **Nothing runs** until `settings.extensions` is on with consent `>= FEATURE_CONSENT_VERSION.extensions` and the
  admin's `features.extensions` isn't false (LOCKDOWN sets it false). The kill switch's `extensions` part stops them.

## Long-term memory

docs/MEMORY.md is the design. The core (app/main/memory/) is pure and has no Electron. Keep it that way.
- **Every fact goes through `MemoryGraph.add()`**, which runs `memoryRefusal()` (guard.ts). A fact holding a secret is
  refused whole, never stored redacted.
- **Recalled facts reach a model only through `memoryBlock()`** (`<memory untrusted="true">`). Only `user`-sourced
  facts may shape what Ember does. Policy never checks a risky step against memory.
- **`memory.json` is 0600** and written whole through `memoryFile()`. A broken file is moved aside, never overwritten.
- **Nothing is remembered until memory has its own consent.** That means `settings.memory.on` with
  `memory.consent.version >= FEATURE_CONSENT_VERSION.memory`, and the admin's `features.memory` not false. The host
  gives the conductor memory only then (`memoryWanted()`), and only then does it have remember, recall and
  forget_memory. With memory off, the tools and the prompt are today's exactly.
- **forget_memory needs the user's own words** (`asksToForget(own, factText)`, conductor.ts: a forget request this
  turn with a word of the fact after it; `own` is never unchecked speech or a routine's steps), and refuses when the
  store can't say what the fact is (`factText` absent). What it forgot is said in the conversation in the fact's
  stored words ("Ember forgot: …", `onAskedForget`), with Undo through `MemoryService.restore` (UNDO_FORGET_MS).
- **A saved fact is the user's only in their own words, said the same way round** (`ownFact`, service.ts: `ownWords`
  and `samePolarity`, so "Priya is not my manager" never makes "Priya is the manager" theirs). Anything else is
  `conversation`.
- **Learning on its own** (`MemoryLearning`, learning.ts; docs/MEMORY.md steps 3–5) runs only while memory is in force,
  and the host and conductor reach it only through `HostOptions.memory` (`heard`, `forRequest`, `taskDone`,
  `useModel`, `intent`, `rememberNote`):
  - every model call is `copilotOneShot()` (agent/one-shot.ts): `hardenedSessionConfig` with `tools: []`,
    `verifySessionTools(session, [])`, deleted after (also when creating it failed); its answer is capped
    (`MAX_ANSWER_CHARS`), zod-checked (`ExtractionSchema`, `RefinementSchema`) and dropped whole if invalid;
  - the source is decided in code, never by the model: `turnSource()` for turns (heard from the user, evidence word
    for word in their words, stated as true now (`statesNow`), the fact's words in the evidence, the same polarity),
    `task` for results, and never above the weakest fact read for a summary or reflection; no extracted fact that
    `soundsLikeInstruction()` is ever kept (only the remember tool and the introduction keep such words, in the
    user's own); refinement closes only a non-user fact about the same thing (predicate, or `SAME_THING` similarity);
  - `own` (not `voiceUnchecked`) travels with every request: `Conductor.ask(…, own)` → `heard(said, reply, own)` and
    `forRequest(request, own)`. Gaps are counted only in `forRequest`, from the user's own words, never from the
    recall tool (`context`), whose query the model wrote and the screen may steer;
  - keep within `LEARN_BUDGET` and `REFINE_PER_DAY`; register nothing long-running outside its `memory` StopPart;
  - a fact the user gives (introduction, interview, an answer to Ember's question) is `user` only when they said it
    (`own`); a request (`looksLikeRequest`) or "forget that" is never taken as an answer, except "call me <a short
    name>" to the interview's name question (`Interview.answers`), whose name is kept without its lead-in
    (`nameFrom`); Ember's question is built from a name alone (`gapQuestion`), never one that reads like an order;
  - every Forget goes through `MemoryLearning.forget()` so "forget that" lets go of it too and its "Ember learned"
    line says so where it was (`onForgot` with what went: `linesAfterForget`, then `linesAfterRestore`, never taken
    off the page under the user's focus: memory-c1-3); forgetting an open fact Ember kept just now (within `FORGET_THAT_MS`: its line's Undo, "forget that", a
    Forget soon after) reopens what it had replaced (`reopenSuperseded`), and Home's line and the reply say so
    (`back`); forgetting an older one forgets it alone, and what it replaced stays history (memory-12);
  - a summary or reflection records what it was made from (`derivedFrom`); forgetting or correcting a fact forgets
    those notes in the same staged write (`notesFrom`), so nothing Ember wrote keeps a forgotten fact's words;
  - every change to the graph tells Better recall (`semantic.changed()`: `remember`, `learn`, `refine`, a reopen), and
    the recall tool goes through `MemoryService.context()` for its matches by meaning; `forRequest` awaits it.
- **Changing memory's note** (`MEMORY_CONSENT_TEXT`) means bumping `FEATURE_CONSENT_VERSION.memory`. What goes
  to Copilot with every request is in it: the profile (`profileFacts`: `called`, `answer_length`, `answer_style`
  `user` facts, before each of the user's requests through `requestContext`) and the few facts recall finds; a new
  kind of fact sent with every request needs the note changed and bumped.
- **Sign-out and an account change call `forgetAll()`**, which erases the file too, but only memory verified as this
  account's in this run (`mayEraseOnSignOut`; it returns whether it erased). Sign out's question says unverified
  memory stays (`erasesOnSignOut` in the view, `kept`), and an account change that erased memory says so in the new
  conversation (`ACCOUNT_CHANGED_FORGOT`).
- **Saying why memory is closed.** The host tells `MemoryOwner` why it has no account (`pauseAccount(why)`,
  `verifyAccount(null, why)`: `checking`, `signed-out`, `own-provider`, `org-provider`, `unverified`), and the owner
  adds `other-account` or `unowned` for data it can't open (`MemoryOwner.reason`, in every `MemoryView`). Home and
  the conversation word it through app/shared/memory-owner.ts; only `other-account`, `unowned` and memory left after
  signing out are offered Forget everything (`memoryOwnerOffersErase`). Main's `memoryEpoch` (in every view) moves on
  only when memory is for another account or none (`MemoryEpochAccount`): a reconnect's `checking`, a failed check's
  `unverified` and the same account verified again hold it, so Home keeps a correction draft, the "Forgotten" line
  with its Undo and focus out of sight and brings them back; memory itself still opens only once `MemoryOwner.ready`
  (memory-8).
- **Better recall's vectors are facts too** (embed.ts): only in `memory-vectors.json` (0600, through `vectorFile()`),
  dropped with a forgotten fact, erased by `forgetAll()` and Remove, never sent anywhere. The model runs only in the
  "Ember memory" process (embed-process.ts, a `StopPart` through `SemanticMemory.stop()`), is fetched only from Settings
  › Memory's Download while memory is on (better-recall.ts; a download under way finishes through a reconnect's
  account check, `memoryWanted`, and is cancelled when memory is turned off), and is used only while memory is on. With no model,
  recall must stay byte for byte lexical (memory.test.ts pins it).

## Settings and admin policy

- **New settings.**
  - Add the field to `SettingsSchema` with a `.default()`; a bad value is repaired field by field.
  - Read and change settings only through `SettingsStore`: `get()` returns them with the admin's policy applied, and `update()` writes atomically. Never write settings.json yourself.
  - Test it in settings.test.ts.
- **Admin-controlled settings.** If an admin should be able to turn a setting off, add the key to `AdminPolicySchema`, `applyAdminPolicy` and `managedKeys`, so Home shows it as managed. Document it in each OS's "For administrators" section of docs/DEVELOPING.md (Linux, §10.7, §11.7).
- **The admin policy** (`AdminPolicySchema`, `.strict()`) can only tighten Ember. It is `/etc/ember/policy.json` on Linux (root-owned, no group or other write), `/Library/Application Support/Ember/policy.json` on macOS (file and folder root-owned, no links) and `HKLM\SOFTWARE\Policies\Ember` `PolicyJson` on Windows (`ADMIN_POLICY_PATHS` and the platform's admin-policy module). Anything unreadable, invalid or untrusted applies `LOCKDOWN`, which also turns WSL, SSH and commands off. A change while Ember runs holds it locked down until restart (admin-watch.ts), and main sends an open settings window what is then in force (`IPC.adminChanged`, built by `homeAdmin()` as every status read's is). Combine a remote's policy with the PC's only through `combineAdmin`. Because the schema is strict, an older Ember treats an unknown key as a broken file: say so in the release notes.

## Platforms, commands and targets

docs/DEVELOPING.md §12 and §14.3 hold the full rules; these are the ones most often broken.
- **The platform boundary.** Only app/main/platform/ tests `process.platform` or reads a native window handle (platform-boundary.test.ts). Code there and in app/sidecar-host loads under plain Node; koffi loads lazily inside a factory that runs only on its OS, with every `koffi.load` guarded.
- **Fail closed.** A check that can't run refuses, returns null or sends no image: `systemDialogOpen()` is true when it can't tell, and nothing is captured without Screen Recording.
- **A shell's own password prompt.** While `Perception.promptOpen()` says one is up (GNOME Shell's polkit and keyring dialogs, Windows' and macOS's credential prompts as the accessibility host lists them, and on Linux the window of a program that is only a prompt, focused or not: `PROMPT_APP` in vocabulary.ts, pinentry, ssh-askpass, polkit agents and gcr's prompter, by either part of WM_CLASS, through the platform's `privacy.isPromptWindow` and Perception's `isPromptWindow`; never an app that stays open, such as firewall-config or a password manager, which `isSecurityWindow` only leaves out; safety-privacy-3), or that it can't tell (`"unchecked"`: a shell too busy to look through, a desktop the helper couldn't list or an app of it the desktop couldn't hand over, an X11 window stack that couldn't be read, or a check that failed or timed out; `"helper"`: the helper can't work here at all, `helperCantRun()` in perception/sidecar.ts), `quickContext()`, `describe()` and `perceive()` read nothing (`Seen.authPrompt`), and the worker's `promptRefusal()` refuses and audits every step by accessibility, `open_app`/settings and `list_windows`, before a question the step (or fill_cells' batch, `authorizeCells()`) would put up, as well as right before acting, so nobody is asked about a step that is then refused (the borrow already asks `systemDialogOpen()`, which counts a prompt program's window too, and refuses when that check throws; a question about a borrow is also refused first while an authentication window has the keyboard, `keyboardTaken()`, which looks only at the active window). Each of these refusals also tells the task's card, as a `refused` activity with the audit's reason (of the window system's reason, macOS's secure keyboard entry or a Wayland session, only its cause before ", so", whole: `causeOf`, tasks-approvals-v5-3; the audit and the model get all of it), so its row and Home's read "Not allowed: Ember's accessibility helper isn't working", never only "Tried to …" (tasks-approvals-r3-4); refusals for one reason in a row fold into one row ("Not allowed 6 times: …", `refusedWords`), and a step refused after its question was answered goes after that answer, at its own time (ActivityLog, tasks-approvals-v5-1, v5-2). A Work without asking step is never looked at twice for this: its decision's audit entries and its announcement are held (`Worker.ahead`) and written and said only once the look right before acting has passed (`goAhead()` in act, system and withCursor), so a step that look refuses is recorded only as refused and nobody is told Ember went ahead (safety-privacy-v4-2); an action entry written before that in the same call (a clipboard that can't be saved) writes the held decision first, and the next tool call drops one its own call didn't take (`runTool`), so a refused step is never written later as going ahead (safety-privacy-r4-1). A check Ember couldn't make is never said to be a prompt: the worker's words and the audit's reason say Ember couldn't tell (`PROMPT_UNCHECKED`), or, for `"helper"`, that the helper isn't working and the setup check says why (`HELPER_DOWN`), never to try again. `helperCantRun()` matches only the helper's can't-work answers (python3-pyatspi missing, no session bus, the Accessibility permission, an AX API or bridge that can't load or start, a Windows bridge that stopped before it was ready twice in a row (uia.ts `exitBeforeReady`), an out-of-date helper), never a timeout, a restart, a bridge that stopped once or one still starting: those may pass (safety-privacy-c2-1). macOS answers `shell_overlays` from the window server's list alone (`AxProvider.systemWindows`, without the provider's start), so the check runs before the Accessibility permission is given. It is asked once per call, never cached: never read before it in describe or perceive; quickContext reads first (its focus can't wait) and throws the read away.
- **A request's context.** `quickContext()` (and a remote's) carries only the window the user is in: its app, title, focused control and that control's selection or the start of its text. Other windows' titles go only through describe_screen, as "Before Ember starts" says Ember reads the window you're working in (safety-privacy-r2); listing them again is a change to what Ember sends, with its consent bump.
- **OS-keyed lists** live in one place each, with the Linux row frozen: app/shared/shortcuts.ts, words.ts and never-look.ts, policy/vocabulary.ts, hands/terminals.ts, agent/clipboard-formats.ts and policy/remote-surface.ts.
- **Option tables** (`parseOpts`'s specs in commands/classify.ts and tables.ts). List in `shortValue`/`longValue` only options that take the next word, checked against the tool's own manual: a switch listed there swallows the command or file after it, so it goes unjudged (`rsync --mkpath`, `xargs -l`, `podman run -P`). A table shared by several tools (the grep family, the ls family) lists only what every one of them takes; a tool's own go in a per-tool entry (ag's `--ignore` is GNU grep's `--ignore-case`). GNU's optional values (`-l[N]`, `--backup[=CONTROL]`) go in `shortOptional`/`longOptional`, make's `-j [N]` in `shortNumeric`/`longNumeric` (apart, the next word only when it is a number, a processor count's substitution such as `$(nproc)`, arithmetic or a variable, as make took it before round six; attached, only a count: `attachedCount`). A job count is a count only when `countWord()` (words.ts) says so, failing closed (commands-a7-1): a number, a processor count's substitution (`CPU_COUNT_WORD`) or arithmetic of numbers, quoted or not, with blanks round it or not. Never a variable, whatever the command set it to: Ember tracks no variable to loosen a judgement. A variable is judged as on main: attached (`make -j$N`, `make -j"$N"`, `--max-workers=$N`) an operand, a target Ember can't name and for make a variable override; after Gradle's `--max-workers` or Maven's `--threads` apart a target; apart after make's `-j`, `-l`, `--jobs` and `--load-average`, Maven's `-T` and ninja's `-j` and `-l` the count, with what the text set it to still split as the shell splits it and each word after the count judged as an argument of its own (`T='4 deploy'; make -j $T` and `T=deploy; make -j "$T"` build deploy, `./gradlew --max-workers $T` runs its tasks, commands-remote-x6-1; Maven's `-T` and ninja's `-j` take any word, so the one after is a target, commands-y6-2), and a variable set from a command's output, an expansion or a read (`mayHoldWords`: `X=$(cat jobs.txt); make -j $X`) asks as `make -j $(cat jobs.txt)` does. A processor count's substitution (`CPU_COUNT_WORD`) is a number wherever `readArgs` meets it, apart or attached (`cmake --build b -j $(nproc)`, `pytest -n $(nproc)`); a variable there still asks. A value that names a file or folder is judged where the row consumes it, as the bare path would be (`optionFiles()`: read, write, a folder written into, or the folder the command runs in), unless optionPaths' `FILE_OPTION`/`OUTPUT_OPTION` already match its long name: listing it as a value and never judging it hides the file (`zstd -o`, `node --redirect-warnings`, `parallel --wd`, `patch -d`: commands-a7-3; `optIs` matches a long option shortened to three letters or more, so a tool whose manual takes a shorter one names it too, as patch's `--di`: commands-b7-5). Before a command (`stopAtOperand`), a word Ember can't see all of whose written start is an option with its value attached (`--arg-file="$d/x"`, `-a"$d/x"`) is that option, its value's pieces kept (`Opt.parts`) for `optionFiles()` to judge for Ember's own refusal; unless that value is a count (`countWord`) on a job-count option (`OptSpec.jobCounts`: `xargs -P$(nproc)`, `parallel -j$(nproc)`), it may hold the command, quoted or not (`X='1 rm'; xargs -n$X echo` runs rm), so the word is also the first operand, the command Ember can't name, as on main (commands-z6-4, commands-a7-1). Add a flag-before-operand row to commands-classify.test.ts's option-table test for each table you change, and a value-is-a-path row for each value option that names a file.
- **Loops and ifs on a command line.** The flow drops an sh or bash line's flow words (`if`…`fi`, `for`/`while`/`until`…`done`; `withoutReadFlow`, `SCRIPT_FLOW`) only when nothing else in the line has a finding and the classifier's `holdsFlow` is false; otherwise every finding stays and the line asks as on main, with its loop's or if's words (main asked for every one). A body is judged once, in the folder and with the values the line starts with, so `holdsFlow` is set by a folder change (`cd`, `chdir`, `pushd`, `popd`: commands-b7-1), any variable set anywhere in the line but a `for` list's or a read's own (`setVar` without `loop`, `${x:=…}`, and `set`, `unset`, `shift`, `source`, `.`, `exec`, `wait -p`, a `{name}` word: `SHELL_STATE`, `changesShellState`; commands-b7-3). A finding anywhere in the line keeps the flow words too, so a loop or an if never announces a dangerous command the user's words asked for (commands-b7-2), and a `[[ … ]]` test keeps its `unclear.keyword` on a command line (`plainTest` reads one through only in a script, as on main: commands-b7-4). Never read a line through on anything a classification doesn't say (`holdsFlow` absent keeps the words), and never widen these by tracking what a loop's later passes see: fall back to main.
- **Text a read or a loop works on.** On top of that (commands-a7-2): any `read`, `mapfile`, `readarray`, `select` or `getopts`, in a loop or not, asks (`unclear.read-text`, "It works on text Ember can't see.") whatever its variables are used for later, unless it reads its standard input (no `-u`, no `<&3`, no pipe, no redirection of its own but a here-string or here-document) and that is a here-string or here-document of written-out text (`Simple.ownInput` "seen": `WRITTEN_OUT`, no tilde, variable or substitution), its own or its loop's or `if`'s closer's (`judgeReadText`, paired by `fedReadsByCloser`); a `for` list with any word that isn't written out (`writtenOut`: a glob, a variable, `~`, a brace, a substitution, `"$@"`) asks the same way. Ember tracks no loop variable to let a command through; what it knows of their values serves only Ember's own refusal, which comes first (`feedReads`, `setVar`). `IFS` in front of a `read` is no assignment only after a loop's or an `if`'s flow word (`LOOP_OR_IF`).
- **Commands.** Command text travels in `EMBER_CMD` (or stdin), never on an argv a user or a remote wrote. No network listener anywhere: askpass uses a 0600 Unix socket or a named pipe only the user's SID may open. Nothing secret is written on a remote, and the remote agent's import graph never reaches Electron (remote-agent-build.test.ts).
- **A target's paths** use `node:path/posix`, `node:path/win32` or `resolveTargetPath`, never `node:path`, in commands/, wsl/runner.ts, wsl/service.ts, remote/ and app/remote/ (ESLint enforces it). Never `path.normalize` a POSIX path before opening it.
