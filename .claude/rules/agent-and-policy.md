# Agent, policy and safety: how the rules are implemented

The root CLAUDE.md states the safety rules. This file says where each one lives and how to extend it without weakening it. Every rule here traces to PLAN.md §5 and the §6 gates:
- the kill switch stops everything in under 300 ms;
- a borrow is aborted in under 100 ms;
- injection fixtures produce no out-of-scope actions;
- no risky action goes unconfirmed, measured in Interactive, the default and the eval's mode: a step Assisted's check cleared or Autopilot announced is audited with its mode (and Assisted's verdict) and isn't counted as unconfirmed (2026-11-12, settled by the integrator on the owner's direction; the owner can overrule: PLAN.md §10).

The eval judges its two blocking safety gates (out-of-scope actions on the injection fixtures, and risky actions without confirmation) from each attempt's audit log. It times the kill switch and the borrow abort directly, and tracks them without blocking.

## Copilot sessions and prompts

- **Hardening.** Build every session with `hardenedSessionConfig()`, then call `verifySessionTools()` (app/main/agent/session-config.ts). That gives `availableTools: []`, `excludedBuiltinAgents: BUILTIN_AGENTS`, and an `onPreToolUse` that denies any tool Ember didn't grant.
- **System prompt.** Use `customize` mode through `emberSections()` (persona.ts). Never replace or remove the platform safety section, and put tone inside the identity replacement.
- **Model-facing text.** Describe worker behaviour in `workerRules()`/`commandsRule()` and guide behaviour in `GUIDE_RULES` (worker-persona.ts), and conductor behaviour in `toolRules()` (persona.ts). Take each OS's words from `PLATFORM_WORDS`. `WORKER_RULES` and `TOOL_RULES` are the frozen Linux outputs: with commands off everywhere (`NO_CAPS`), every prompt stays byte-identical (persona-commands.test.ts).

## Adding or changing a worker tool

- **Definition.** Define the tool with `this.tool()` in worker.ts. It parses the zod arguments (the runtime does not), counts the 150-step limit, and runs one call at a time.
- **Routing actions.** Send every app action through `act()` or `system()`. These call `authorize()`, which asks `PolicyEngine.decide()`, asks the user when needed, and writes the audit entries. Commands go only through `run_command` → app/main/commands/flow.ts (the classifier, then decide.ts), never through `authorize()`.
- **Refusals and errors.** Refuse through `refuse()` (logged as a `decision`), return `denied(…)` for a refusal and `fail(…)` for an error. A handler that throws anyway is caught in `runTool()`: logged as `worker.tool_failed` with the tool, and told to the model by `toolFailure()` (the accessibility helper's answers that it can't work here, `helperCantRun()`, point to the setup check; anything else, a timeout or a restart included, is quoted as untrusted words).
- **The minute's limit paces, never refuses.** A decision past the actions-a-minute limit is `paced` (verdict deny, so nothing acts, and it isn't counted); `authorize()` and `authorizeCells()` wait in `pace()` (`PolicyEngine.paceMs()`, on `overSignal`, the card's live line saying so) and decide afresh. Never more than `perMinute` actions go through in any 60 s.
- **Stopping.** After every `await`, check `if (this.isOver) return STOPPED();`, so a stop takes effect at once.
- **Wiring.**
  - Decide whether guide mode gets the tool (the `guide` set in `tools()`).
  - Give it bubble words in `TOOL_STEPS` (presenter.ts) and a kind of step in `STEP_GROUP` (app/shared/activity.ts: its card's words), and describe it in the persona rules. Its card hears of it through the worker's `onActivity` (app/main/agent/activity.ts): tool calls, questions, refusals and notes, never arguments or screen text.
  - Keep eval/tasks/*.json calling it with arguments it accepts (eval-harness.test.ts checks this).
- **Tests.** Test it in worker-tools.test.ts and policy.test.ts (worker-commands.test.ts for command tools).

## Policy and confirmation

- **Decisions.** Describe each action as an `ActionRequest`, and let `PolicyEngine.decide()` (app/main/policy/policy.ts) decide it. Build question text from the request (`describe(r)`), never from model output.
- **Matching.** Normalise labels with `normalizeLabel`/`foldLabel` before matching word lists. Policy checks a risky step against `TaskSpec.userWords`, never against the conductor's goal string. A "try again" (`retryRequest`, status.ts: also "can you try again?", "yes, try again") after the line of a task that may be tried again (Ember's lines and at most two short questions of the user's about what happened between), or a yes to that task's own offer to try again, carries that task's own words and what they agreed to (`TaskManager.askedWith`, host.ts `retriedWords`), tells the new worker when the old try did part of the work (`partWay`: a step that changed something, never only a look or a picture of the window, `LOOKS_ONLY` in tasks.ts) and closes the old card: never its goal, never for unchecked or suggested words, never after a "Not now" or a Stop, never past a request of the user's own.
- **New risky verbs.** Add each one to `RISKY` and to the per-language `RISKY_WORDS`/`RISKY_ANYWHERE` lists (verbs that only a control acts on: `RISKY_ACT*`; one OS's words: `RISKY_EXTRA[os]` in vocabulary.ts; a bare "Disable" or "Turn off" on a window or dialog titled for a protection: `TURNS_OFF` with `PROTECTION_TITLE`). A word that is a noun too goes in `RISKY_ACT_LEAD` with its look-ahead ("Book", "Book a table"; not "Bookmarks"); an everyday word or phrase that grants or commits goes in `COMMITS_LEAD_WORDS` or `COMMITS_PHRASES` (parts of `RISKY_ACT_LEAD` and `RISKY_ACT`, whose sources must stay as they are). A link, tab or menu item asked about only for a word of `COMMITS_LEAD_WORDS` leading its name (`commitsOnly`: never a `COMMITS_PHRASES` phrase, which grants rights, changes who can see something, drops work or turns a protection off, nor a grant of access, `GRANTS_ACCESS`: "Allow", "Permit", "Allow notifications", "Allow once" ("Allow Editing" and the headline "Permit applications open" are spared), safety-privacy-x8-4; not RISKY's lists, the older acting verbs `ACT_LEAD_BEFORE`/`RISKY_ACT_BEFORE`, another language's or security talk) still asks every time, but only after every older rule has had its say, and then as `COMMITS_RISK`, which `judge()` never calls unasked (no "You didn't ask for this", no taint): those words also lead headlines, tabs and settings (safety-privacy-x7-1). ## Untrusted text, secrets and never-look windows

- **Untrusted text.** Wrap text from the screen, apps, files, the web or other tasks in an element marked `untrusted="true"`: `<screen>`, `<context>`, `<windows>`, `<options>`, `<task_results>`, `<tasks>` or `<command_output>` (built only by `shapeOutput()`). Quote it with `quoteUntrusted()`, and escape role and action names with `screenWord()`.
- **Secrets.** `SECRET_WORDS`/`SECRET_ANYWHERE` must stay the same in TypeScript and Python. The Windows and macOS host (app/sidecar-host/secrets.ts) imports elements.ts's lists: never copy them there. Add new labels to test/fixtures/secret-labels.json (and options to chosen-options.json), because both test suites read these files.
- **Never-look windows.** Check the never-look list (`settings.privacy.neverLook` plus the admin's list) before every capture and every tree read. Those windows are left out of trees and window lists, and blacked out in images.
- **Screenshots.** Only workers get them, and only while the model reads images, the admin's `features.screenImages` isn't false and the user's `settings.screenImages` (Home's "Send screenshots for tasks") is on (host.ts `workerVision`, read as each task's worker is made, and the worker's `picturesAllowed()`, read again at every look, so turning it off stops a task already at work). If `maskAfter` returns null, send no image. Each one is audited before it is sent (`event: "read"`, `kind: "screenshot"`: the app and its size, never the window's title), and one that can't be recorded isn't sent; What Ember did lists it, and the task's card says it (the worker's `picture` activity makes its read row "Sent the model a picture of the window", named first in the summary). Keep it out of `event: "action"`, which the eval's safety gates count.
- **Launching apps on the user's screen.** Use only the platform's `AppCatalog.launch()`: on Linux `launchApp()` (app/main/hands/system.ts: `gtk-launch <desktop id>`, else the parsed Exec line, `execArgv`, no shell), on Windows explorer.exe with the app's shortcut, on macOS `open -a`. Each refuses terminals (`isTerminalApp(app, os)`), starts in the home folder and passes `appEnv()`, an allowlist that never passes tokens, keys, secrets or passwords, or Ember's, GitHub's, Copilot's, SSH's, GPG's, the keyring's or Electron's variables.
- **Launching apps on the desk.** Apps start through the desk place's `open()` (app/main/desk/place.ts). It also refuses terminals, and calls `Desk.run()` with the desk's `appEnv(process.env, { session: false })` environment and the hand-off folder.
- **Ember's own windows.** Ember never reads or acts in them: `isEmberWindow()` (policy.ts) matches Ember's process id, then its app name, and its titles (`EMBER_WINDOW`) only when the id is unknown.

## Tiers, the desk and the kill switch

- **Tier 1** (background accessibility: AT-SPI on Linux and in WSL, UI Automation or AX through app/sidecar-host) never moves the pointer, takes focus or raises a window. Return `background_unavailable` instead, and let the router ask to escalate.
- **Tier 3** (borrowing the cursor):
  - an ordinary borrow is announced instead of asked in every mode (`waivable: ["cursor"]`), a risky one asks as its mode says, and below Interactive every borrow asks;
  - wait for the user to be still;
  - let go on any user input, and after that never move the pointer again;
  - unless the user took over, restore the pointer and the active window when the borrow ends.

  Target-dependent refusals (no borrowing in WSLg windows) live only in policy/target-rules.ts. On the user's screen, text typed with the borrowed keyboard always goes in by paste (any layout, accents, long text), and the clipboard is put back afterwards. Ember pastes only when it can put the clipboard back. On the desk, text is typed.
- **The desk** (Linux only; `Platform.desk` is null elsewhere) is a private Xvfb with its own D-Bus, clipboard and app profiles. Make every desk X call in the desk helper (app/main/desk/agent-process.ts, bundled as dist/app/desk-agent.cjs and run with `ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE`), never in Ember's own process. Risky steps on the desk are still confirmed. Desks use displays :90 to :139.
- **The kill switch.** Register every new long-running part with `KillSwitch` as a `StopPart`. After a stop, no tool sends anything to any app.
- **Process cleanup.** Never signal a cached pid without checking what it names. Linux desk and command sweeps retain
  `/proc/<pid>/stat` start times and check them again immediately before signalling; unreadable or reused identities
  are left alone. macOS sweeps re-read the target pid and its run marker. The desk relay stops its own live child
  handle, never a pid cached by the main thread.

  - put it in `bubble.interactiveRects()` or `taskRects`;

- **Consent.** Read, hear and send nothing until `hasConsent` is true. - **The microphone.**
  - It opens only when all of these hold: listening for “Hey Ember” is on (`wakeWord`), the admin's policy allows voice (`features.voice`), voice consent and general consent are given, the listening models (`LISTENING_MODELS`) are verified, Ember isn't muted, and the screen isn't locked.

## Extensions

docs/DEVELOPING.md "Extensions: the runtime and the gate" is the design; app/main/extensions/ the code.
- **Only MCP servers load**, passed by Ember as explicit `mcpServers`. Never pass a third-party plugin as
  `pluginDirectories` (its command hooks run outside every gate), never set `requestExtensions`, and never give a model
  a tool that installs, enables, reloads or manages extensions: only the user does that, in Home.
- **Admit tools one by one** through `hardenedSessionConfig({ extensions })`: `mcp:<server>-<tool>` names only, never
  one equal to an Ember tool (an MCP tool replaces it silently), never `builtin:`.
- **Every call goes through `gate.ts`'s `classify()`**: hints only make a call stricter; a changing tool asks every
  time with a question built by `gateQuestion()` (no Always); a deny is final for the turn; every decision and result
  is audited with sizes only; a call that can't be recorded is denied.
- **Results** are rewritten by `wrapResult()` (untrusted, `<` escaped, capped, no images) and scanned by injection.ts.
  Failures can't be rewritten: `onPostToolUseFailure` adds `failureNote()` and taints the conversation. Taint lives on
  the service for the whole conversation (reconnects included), never on one session.
- **Check pins at every call**, against the session's live tool list (`bind`), not only at load: a server can change
  a tool mid-session. A session without a live list denies every call.
- **Read-only** is `isReadOnly()` (readOnlyHint, and neither destructiveHint nor openWorldHint) on a stdio server only;
  a remote server's tools always ask, and so do a server's tools once it changes its tool list mid-session.
- **The runtime's TMPDIR** is `copilot-tmp/` in the data folder (runtime-temp.ts, 0700, emptied at connect and
  sign-out): it saves large tool output there. A result carrying its "Saved to:" pointer counts as flagged.
- **Pins** (pin.ts) are checked at install, start and re-enable, and against what the session shows; a changed tool
  stays off until `reagree`.
- **Servers start through the launcher** (launch.ts) with `appEnv()`'s allowlist, never the runtime's environment.
- **Nothing runs** until `settings.extensions` is on with consent `>= FEATURE_CONSENT_VERSION.extensions` and the
  admin's `features.extensions` isn't false (LOCKDOWN sets it false). The kill switch's `extensions` part stops them.

## Long-term memory

docs/MEMORY.md is the design. The core (app/main/memory/) is pure and has no Electron. Keep it that way.
- **Every fact goes through `MemoryGraph.add()`**, which runs `memoryRefusal()` (guard.ts). A fact holding a secret is
  refused whole, never stored redacted.
- **Recalled facts reach a model only through `memoryBlock()`** (`<memory untrusted="true">`). Only `user`-sourced
  facts may shape what Ember does. Policy never checks a risky step against memory.
- **`memory.json` is 0600** and written whole through `memoryFile()`. A broken file is moved aside, never overwritten.
- **Nothing is remembered until memory has its own consent.** That means `settings.memory.on` with
  `memory.consent.version >= FEATURE_CONSENT_VERSION.memory` (from its note, or from first run's one agreement while
  memory is left on there: owner decision of 2026-11-12), and the admin's `features.memory` not false. The host gives
  the conductor memory only then (`memoryWanted()`), and only then does it have remember, recall and forget_memory.
  With memory off, the tools and the prompt are today's exactly.
- **forget_memory needs the user's own words** (`asksToForget(own, factText)`, conductor.ts: a forget request this
  turn with a word of the fact after it; `own` is never unchecked speech or a routine's steps), and refuses when the
  store can't say what the fact is (`factText` absent). What it forgot is said in the conversation in the fact's
  stored words ("Ember forgot: …", `onAskedForget`), with Undo through `MemoryService.restore` (UNDO_FORGET_MS).
- **A saved fact is the user's only in their own words, said the same way round** (`ownFact`, service.ts: `ownWords`
  and `samePolarity`, so "Priya is not my manager" never makes "Priya is the manager" theirs). Anything else is
  `conversation`.
- **Learning on its own** (`MemoryLearning`, learning.ts; docs/MEMORY.md steps 3–5) runs only while memory is in force,
  and the host and conductor reach it only through `HostOptions.memory` (`asked`, `heard`, `forRequest`, `taskDone`,
  `useModel`, `intent`, `rememberNote`):
  - every model call is `copilotOneShot()` (agent/one-shot.ts): `hardenedSessionConfig` with `tools: []`,
    `verifySessionTools(session, [])`, deleted after (also when creating it failed); its answer is capped
    (`MAX_ANSWER_CHARS`), zod-checked (`ExtractionSchema`, `RefinementSchema`) and dropped whole if invalid;
  - the source is decided in code, never by the model: `turnSource()` for turns (heard from the user, evidence word
    for word in their words, stated as true now (`statesNow`), the fact's words in the evidence, the same polarity),
    `task` for results, and never above the weakest fact read for a summary or reflection; no extracted fact that
    `soundsLikeInstruction()` is ever kept (only the remember tool and the introduction keep such words, in the
    user's own); refinement closes only a non-user fact about the same thing (predicate, or `SAME_THING` similarity);
  - `own` (not `voiceUnchecked`) travels with every request: `Conductor.ask(…, own)` → `heard(said, reply, own)` and
    `forRequest(request, own)`. Gaps are counted only in `forRequest`, from the user's own words, never from the
    recall tool (`context`), whose query the model wrote and the screen may steer;
  - so does the mark `asked()` gives as a request is asked, back to `heard` with its turn: a Forget, a correction and
    Forget everything drop every turn and result said before them (`dropSaid`: waiting, being read or still under
    way; with no mark, any within `FORGOTTEN_TURN_MS`, as every `taskDone` result is), never a similarity of words
    (memory-r8-1); a task the conversation starts carries its request's mark (`Conductor.askedMark`, the host's
    `taskMarks`) back with `taskDone`, which only adds to what is dropped (memory-x8-4); a new path into `Learner`
    carries such a mark or fails closed the same way;
  - what the remember tool kept in a turn goes with that turn (`Learner.heard`'s `kept`) and no other (the conductor's
    `began` at every turn's start lets go of what an unread turn kept: memory-x8-2), and its reading keeps no
    candidate `keptInTurn` calls the same fact, compared on words and on the clause of the turn they were said in;
    `about` only ever tells two apart (a person only one names), and where it can't tell, it learns (memory-c8-1,
    memory-x8-1);
  - keep within `LEARN_BUDGET` and `REFINE_PER_DAY`; register nothing long-running outside its `memory` StopPart;
  - a fact the user gives (introduction, interview, an answer to Ember's question) is `user` only when they said it
    (`own`); a request (`looksLikeRequest`) or "forget that" is never taken as an answer, except "call me <a short
    name>" to the interview's name question (`Interview.answers`), whose name is kept without its lead-in
    (`nameFrom`); Ember's question is built from a name alone (`gapQuestion`), never one that reads like an order;
  - every Forget goes through `MemoryLearning.forget()` so "forget that" lets go of it too and its "Ember learned"
    line says so where it was (`onForgot` with what went: `linesAfterForget`, then `linesAfterRestore`, never taken
    off the page under the user's focus: memory-c1-3); forgetting an open fact Ember kept just now (within `FORGET_THAT_MS`: its line's Undo, "forget that", a
    Forget soon after) reopens what it had replaced (`reopenSuperseded`), and Home's line and the reply say so
    (`back`); forgetting an older one forgets it alone, and what it replaced stays history (memory-12);
  - a summary or reflection records what it was made from (`derivedFrom`); forgetting or correcting a fact forgets
    those notes in the same staged write (`notesFrom`), so nothing Ember wrote keeps a forgotten fact's words;
  - every change to the graph tells Better recall (`semantic.changed()`: `remember`, `learn`, `refine`, a reopen), and
    the recall tool goes through `MemoryService.context()` for its matches by meaning; `forRequest` awaits it.
- **Changing memory's note** (`MEMORY_CONSENT_TEXT`) means bumping `FEATURE_CONSENT_VERSION.memory`. What goes
  to Copilot with every request is in it: the profile (`profileFacts`: `called`, `answer_length`, `answer_style`
  `user` facts, before each of the user's requests through `requestContext`) and the few facts recall finds; a new
  kind of fact sent with every request needs the note changed and bumped.
- **Sign-out and an account change call `forgetAll()`**, which erases the file too, but only memory verified as this
  account's in this run (`mayEraseOnSignOut`; it returns whether it erased). Sign out's question says unverified
  memory stays (`erasesOnSignOut` in the view, `kept`), and an account change that erased memory says so in the new
  conversation (`ACCOUNT_CHANGED_FORGOT`).

- **Better recall's vectors are facts too** (embed.ts): only in `memory-vectors.json` (0600, through `vectorFile()`),

## Settings and admin policy

- **New settings.**
  - Add the field to `SettingsSchema` with a `.default()`; a bad value is repaired field by field.
  - Read and change settings only through `SettingsStore`: `get()` returns them with the admin's policy applied, and `update()` writes atomically. Never write settings.json yourself.
  - Test it in settings.test.ts.
- **Admin-controlled settings.** If an admin should be able to turn a setting off, add the key to `AdminPolicySchema`, `applyAdminPolicy` and `managedKeys`, so Home shows it as managed. Document it in each OS's "For administrators" section of docs/DEVELOPING.md (Linux, §10.7, §11.7). The approval modes are only tightened: `features.autonomy: false`, `allowGrants: false`, `confirmEveryAction`, `features.actions: false` and LOCKDOWN keep Ember below Interactive, `features.aiReview: false` keeps Assisted's check off, and a key that caps the mode, if one is added, goes through the same three places.
- **The admin policy** (`AdminPolicySchema`, `.strict()`) can only tighten Ember. It is `/etc/ember/policy.json` on Linux (root-owned, no group or other write), `/Library/Application Support/Ember/policy.json` on macOS (file and folder root-owned, no links) and `HKLM\SOFTWARE\Policies\Ember` `PolicyJson` on Windows (`ADMIN_POLICY_PATHS` and the platform's admin-policy module). Anything unreadable, invalid or untrusted applies `LOCKDOWN`, which also turns WSL, SSH and commands off. A change while Ember runs holds it locked down until restart (admin-watch.ts), and main sends an open settings window what is then in force (`IPC.adminChanged`, built by `homeAdmin()` as every status read's is). Combine a remote's policy with the PC's only through `combineAdmin`. Because the schema is strict, an older Ember treats an unknown key as a broken file: say so in the release notes.

Wrapper and hook variables that always name a program are in `RISKY_ASSIGNMENTS` (RUSTC_WRAPPER, CARGO_BUILD_RUSTC_WRAPPER, JDK_JAVA_OPTIONS, DOTNET_STARTUP_HOOKS). The same `namesProgram` judgement runs in the `env` row (`env CC='sh -c …' make`), and covers a `cmake -DCMAKE_<lang>_COMPILER=…`/`_LAUNCHER` override, `go test -exec`/`go build -toolexec`/`-vettool`, and npm's `--script-shell`/`config set script-shell` (never `config get`). Each adds `unclear.assignment`, never loosens: a plain path a launcher names (`make CC=/tmp/x`, which main runs) stays as on main; a variable exported in an earlier command (`export CC=… && make`) is not carried, as on main.
- **Deleters, and a program Ember doesn't know given a broad folder** (commands/classify.ts; commands-remote-c8-2). Known deleters take rm's row and its `posix.rm` rule (so the intent row's matching applies): `rimraf`, `del-cli`/`del` (always recursive, `deleteTree`; a word holding `* ? [ ] { } !` is judged as the pattern they expand themselves, quoted or not, unless rimraf's `-G`, and widens `pattern`, so it never announces; with braces or extglob's `(…)`, which the matcher doesn't read, the folder before them is refused when it holds or is one of Ember's folders, and each word the groups stand for, with the braces read as a shell reads them (lex-posix's `braceGroup`), is judged as an operand of its own, refused past `MAX_SIMPLE` of them or with a `{` and a comma that never close, behind which the matcher still expands a group (`patternWords`: `'{..,x}'`, `'+(..)'` and `'{/home/ana,x}'` are refused as `rimraf ..` is; commands-remote-x8-8). rimraf's matcher (minimatch) reads some braces otherwise, a stray `}` before a later comma (`'{a},..,b}'` is `a}`, `..`, `b`) or a `\` in a group (`'{.\.,x}'` is `..`), and del-cli's matcher isn't checked: those patterns only ask, as before, so never say every brace pattern that reaches Ember's files is refused (commands-remote-y8-1)), the Homebrew names of coreutils' own (`grm`, `grmdir`, `gunlink`, `gshred`), `scrub`, `gvfs-rm`, `gvfs-trash`, `rmtrash`, `trash-rm`; `fdupes`/`jdupes` only with `-d` (and jdupes' links); `bfg` and `git-filter-repo` as `git.history`. These names had no row before, so each row calls `asBefore` first (generic(), or an unknown cmdlet's check for a PowerShell word shaped like one: `NEW_DELETERS`), and dispatch and wrappers still read their `-o` as an unknown program's (`optionPaths(h, !n || NEW_DELETERS.has(key))`, so `fdupes -o ~/.bashrc .` asks `path.startup`: commands-remote-x8-5), so none decides less safely than it did, and `del` and `scrub` are in `NOT_RUNNERS`. `yarn`/`pnpm`/`bun <deleter>` and `… run <deleter>` with a word after the name are judged as that program (a payload; npm runs only scripts); with none (`yarn scrub`) it is the project's script or a deleter given nothing, and runs (commands-remote-x8-13); another script name stays a script's. A program with no row (generic() with `noRow`, from dispatch, a wrapper or `asBefore`, after KNOWN_ORDINARY's return; `LISTS_ONLY` listers apart; a row's own fallback to generic(), as busybox's, sponge's or dpkg-deb's, doesn't) given an operand that is the home folder or holds it (the root included) or one that holds Ember's files asks `unclear.broad-folder` (`broadOperand`). A folder above the command's folder alone doesn't: Ember can't tell where a project starts, and after `cd build` (which moves `ctx.cwd`) or with run_command's `cwd`, `qmake ..` is the project itself (commands-remote-x8-7). Option values and install prefixes, attached or apart (`./configure --prefix ~/.local`: `PREFIX_OPTION`), and other folders run.
- **A place in the user's words** (commands/intent.ts; commands-remote-r5-2 with c3-1, i5b, i6b). A row reads only its verb's own phrase (`actedOn`, `objectWords`), which drops what follows "of", "beside", "next to" or a comma, so a place there is read again from all the words before any row runs: `placedElsewhere()` takes every word that names a path object and asks `folderNamed()` where the whole words put it. A place Ember can't check against the path is `ELSEWHERE` and always asks (`elsewhere()`: beside something, on another computer). Add a new way of saying a place there, never to a row. `pathToken()` takes a name written as the command writes it only when the command resolves it in the task's folder (a `cd` first makes it another file).
- **A deletion described as another, or held back** (commands/intent.ts; commands-remote-y7-6). The delete.paths row asks when a word before the path's name (back to a determiner, a verb, a joiner, a preposition, a possessive or another named object) or between it and a following "folder"/"file" word is neither on `ONLY_SAYS_HOW` (old, stale, whole, generated…), a naming word (`NAME_WORDS`: folder, named, called), a filler, nor a segment of the deleted path relative to the task's folder (`describesAnother`): "the production build folder" never covers `rm -rf build`, "the staging build folder" covers `rm -rf staging/build`. Grow `ONLY_SAYS_HOW` only with words that can't pick out one folder among others ("other", "new", "main", "local", "current" pick one). `holdsBack` also holds a step back for each time something happens (`CONDITION`: each, every or any time), a day said alone (`LATER_DAY`: "first thing Monday", "Monday") and a deadline (`deadline()`: before, ahead of, prior to, unless another step of the user's follows, `otherStep` with -ing forms read as the verb, a build tool's command (`toolStep`: `STEP_TOOLS`, and after run or running `RUN_TOOLS`, which adds names that are everyday words such as make and go; the name alone or followed by one of `TOOL_VERBS`, so "before npm 11 ships" is still a deadline), "anything else", "we start" or "that" alone, or it follows "as/like (we did)": commands-remote-x8-9); for the rows that throw something away (`THROWS_AWAY`), a clause before the verb's that asks for a step keeping a copy (`KEEPS_COPY`: back up, copy, archive, commit, stash, push…, or "make a backup") holds it back too (`keepsCopyFirst`). A relative clause after the name ("the old build folder that nobody uses") is still read as describing the same thing, as before. For a recursive deletion the row also wants a folder word by the first word of all the user's words that names the path (`recursionNear` on `whole`), as main read them, so "before you build, delete the build folder" asks (commands-remote-x8-6).
- **Commands.** Command text travels in `EMBER_CMD` (or stdin), never on an argv a user or a remote wrote. No network listener anywhere: askpass uses a 0600 Unix socket or a named pipe only the user's SID may open. Nothing secret is written on a remote, and the remote agent's import graph never reaches Electron (remote-agent-build.test.ts).
- **A target's paths** use `node:path/posix`, `node:path/win32` or `resolveTargetPath`, never `node:path`, in commands/, wsl/runner.ts, wsl/service.ts, remote/ and app/remote/ (ESLint enforces it). Never `path.normalize` a POSIX path before opening it.
